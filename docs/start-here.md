# Delivery Lead Project Journey

Use this guide to orient yourself, keep the project under control, and make stage changes based on evidence. Start at the project's current point; the lifecycle is a guide, not a one-way checklist. Recovery can be needed at any stage.

## How to use the guide

For each action, identify an owner and due date in the project workspace. Link the relevant artifact or decision, update its status as work changes, and raise gaps early. Use the contract, approved methodology, client governance, and project-specific plans to determine which activities and approvals apply. If an activity does not apply, record why and who agreed.

The checks below are prompts, not universal approval gates. The Delivery Lead coordinates readiness; the accountable client, delivery, quality, and business owners make decisions within their roles.

## 1. Mobilize

**Outcome:** The project has a shared understanding of its commitments, people, governance, and immediate next steps.

**DL actions**

- Review the contract, statement of work, assumptions, scope, deliverables, acceptance terms, and constraints.
- Confirm the sponsor, client and delivery leads, team roles, decision owners, and escalation routes.
- Connect the project to its engagement records:
	- In AEP, open the engagement for this project and verify its name or ID against the approved contract or statement of work.
	- In BizApps Platform, open the matching DnA engagement and verify that it refers to the same client and project.
	- Record both engagement links or IDs in the project workspace. If either record is missing, inaccessible, or does not match, assign the correction or access request to the platform owner and track it to resolution.
- Align on the delivery approach, implementation model, environments, tools, and required access.
- Establish the initial plan, milestones, budget and forecast baseline, delivery cadence, and reporting approach.
- Start the RAID log (risks, assumptions, issues, and dependencies), decision log, and change control.
- Identify required onboarding, security, privacy, quality, and compliance activities.

**Evidence to keep:** Contract and assumption review; verified AEP and BizApps Platform DnA engagement links or IDs; team and governance map; initial plan and financial baseline; RAID and decision logs; access and environment actions; agreed cadence.

**Ready to plan when:** Owners know their roles, commitments and assumptions are understood, the team can start its planned work, key controls are in place, and unresolved blockers have owners and escalation dates.

## 2. Plan and analyze

**Outcome:** Scope and solution direction are understood well enough to plan and control delivery.

**DL actions**

- Baseline scope, requirements, priorities, acceptance criteria, and exclusions with the appropriate stakeholders.
- Coordinate solution and implementation planning, including architecture, integrations, data, security, environments, and non-functional needs as applicable.
- Build a sequenced delivery plan with dependencies, milestones, estimates, roles, and client responsibilities.
- Confirm how quality, testing, deployment, change, training, and operational readiness will be handled.
- Reconcile the plan and forecast with contractual commitments; raise gaps or proposed changes through governance.
- Review risks, assumptions, issues, dependencies, and decisions with owners on the agreed cadence.

**Evidence to keep:** Approved scope and requirements baseline; solution decisions; estimates and delivery plan; responsibility assignments; quality and test approach; updated RAID, decision, change, and financial records.

**Ready to execute when:** Scope and acceptance are clear enough to start, dependencies and responsibilities are visible, the plan is credible against commitments, and required reviews or approvals are recorded.

## 3. Execute delivery

**Outcome:** Work is delivered in controlled increments, with current status and timely decisions.

**DL actions**

- Run the agreed delivery and governance cadence; report progress against the baseline using evidence.
- Track scope, milestones, forecast, capacity, quality, and client dependencies; make impacts visible early.
- Keep risks, issues, assumptions, dependencies, decisions, and changes current with owners and target dates.
- Route scope or plan changes through the agreed approval process before treating them as committed work.
- Coordinate build, reviews, testing, defect resolution, deployment planning, and stakeholder communications as applicable.
- Escalate decisions and blockers at the level where they can be resolved; record outcomes and actions.

**Evidence to keep:** Current plan and forecast; status reports; delivery and quality results; approved changes; RAID and decision history; actions and acceptance evidence.

**Ready to continue or transition when:** Progress is supported by evidence, material variances have owners and actions, required quality checks are met, and the next work or decision is understood by the accountable stakeholders.

## 4. Prepare for a phase transition

**Outcome:** The current phase is closed or accepted as required, and the next phase can start with known conditions.

**DL actions**

- Confirm the phase's required deliverables, acceptance criteria, and approval owners from the contract and approved methodology.
- Check completion and quality evidence; distinguish accepted work from open items, defects, and exclusions.
- Review remaining risks, dependencies, decisions, financial position, and resource needs with the next-phase owners.
- Agree any conditions, owners, target dates, and escalation path for work that remains open.
- Obtain and record the required client and internal approvals; update plans and stakeholders with the outcome.

**Evidence to keep:** Deliverable and acceptance records; quality and test results; open-item plan; updated RAID, plan, and forecast; recorded transition decision.

**Ready to transition when:** Required approvals are recorded, outstanding items have explicit disposition and ownership, and the receiving team understands the state, risks, and next commitments.

## 5. Recover or escalate

**Outcome:** A delivery concern is contained, understood, and being addressed through an agreed decision path.

**DL actions**

- State the issue and impact plainly, including effects on scope, dates, cost, quality, people, and contractual commitments.
- Stabilize immediate risks and identify the facts, root causes, dependencies, and decisions needed.
- Bring the right decision owners together; agree options, trade-offs, actions, owners, and dates.
- Update the forecast and recovery plan; make any proposed baseline change through governance.
- Communicate the agreed position and review progress at a cadence suited to the severity.

**Evidence to keep:** Escalation summary; impact assessment; decision record; recovery actions and revised forecast; stakeholder communications.

**Ready to return to normal control when:** Decision owners accept the path forward, urgent risks are contained, actions have owners and dates, and progress can be measured against the recovery plan. Keep escalation active if these conditions are not met.

## 6. Close and hand over

**Outcome:** Delivery obligations are concluded or explicitly handed over, and the receiving owners can operate what was delivered.

**DL actions**

- Confirm contractual deliverables, acceptance, deployment or transition status, and any remaining obligations.
- Coordinate operational handover, support ownership, documentation, knowledge transfer, and access changes as applicable.
- Close or transfer open defects, risks, decisions, actions, and dependencies with named owners and dates.
- Reconcile final financials, resources, and project records; follow the required retention and access rules.
- Capture lessons learned and share them through the appropriate project or organizational channel.

**Evidence to keep:** Acceptance and handover records; support and ownership details; open-item disposition; final financial and project status; lessons learned.

**Ready to close when:** Acceptance or agreed disposition is recorded, operational ownership is clear, remaining obligations are tracked, and project records are complete per organizational policy.

## Keep control throughout

Use the project's agreed cadence to review:

- Scope, milestones, forecast, and change requests.
- Risks, issues, assumptions, dependencies, decisions, and actions.
- Team capacity, client responsibilities, quality, and acceptance evidence.
- Governance, contractual commitments, and the next readiness decision.

At each review, confirm what changed, what needs a decision, who owns the next action, and when it is due. Keep project records in the approved workspace and link them from reports rather than copying sensitive or fast-changing project data into this repository.
